RE: Masterplan Area Acid Sulphate Soil

I have been requested to provide commentary on the potential presence of Acid Sulfate Soils

within the Mosman Masterplan Area. Reference is made to the draft Mosman Masterplan Structure

Plan prepared by SJB Architecture, dated 3 August 2026 (Drawing No. 06, Revision 08).

This assessment is informed by more than ten years of experience in the review and assessment

of Development Applications within Mosman, including the evaluation of numerous geotechnical

investigations, borehole logs, site classifications and subsurface assessments. During my tenure at

Mosman Council, I have held the positions of Development Engineer, Manager Engineering,

Director Planning and Environment, and now General Manager.

It is further informed by my qualifications in Geology, Geophysics and Geotechnical Engineering.

Prior to joining Mosman Council, I worked in the private sector as a Geophysicist and Geotechnical

Engineer for approximately two years.

A review of the Mosman Local Environmental Plan (LEP) 2012 Acid Sulfate Soils Maps indicates

that the Masterplan area is not identified as either an Acid Sulfate Soil Area or an associated

Buffer Area. The mapped acid sulfate soil constraints within Mosman are generally confined to low-

lying foreshore, estuarine and harbour-edge environments adjacent to Sydney Harbour and Middle

Harbour, and do not extend into the elevated town centre precinct encompassed by the Masterplan

area.

From a geological perspective, the Masterplan area occupies the elevated Mosman ridge system,

generally exceeding RL 60 m AHD, and is therefore significantly removed from the low-lying

estuarine environments typically associated with the formation of acid sulfate soils.

Based on my review of numerous borehole logs and geotechnical investigations undertaken

throughout Mosman over the years, the Masterplan area is not underlain by estuarine sediments.

Rather, it is predominantly underlain by Hawkesbury Sandstone bedrock of the Sydney Basin, with

weathered sandstone profiles and localised occurrences of Mittagong Formation materials forming

transitional units between the Hawkesbury Sandstone and overlying shale sequences.

Geotechnical investigations undertaken across the Mosman town centre consistently encounter a

subsurface profile comprising:



fill and pavement layers associated with historical urban development and infrastructure

works;



residual sandy clay, clayey sand and highly weathered sandstone derived from in-situ

weathering of the underlying bedrock;



moderately to highly weathered Hawkesbury Sandstone; and



fresh to slightly weathered Hawkesbury Sandstone bedrock.

Where fill is present, it is typically urban fill comprising sandstone fragments, sandy gravels,

imported sandy soils, road base materials and miscellaneous construction materials associated

with historical development activities. Such materials are fundamentally different from estuarine or

marine sediments and do not provide the geological conditions typically associated with actual or

potential acid sulfate soils.

Accordingly, having regard to:



the Mosman LEP 2012 Acid Sulfate Soils Mapping;



the elevated topographic position of the Masterplan area;



the absence of estuarine, marine or tidal sediments;



the predominance of Hawkesbury Sandstone bedrock and weathered sandstone profiles;

and



the extensive borehole and geotechnical information reviewed throughout my

professional experience within Mosman,

it is my opinion that the likelihood of encountering actual or potential acid sulfate soils within the

Mosman Masterplan Area is negligible. Consequently, no significant planning, geotechnical or

environmental constraints associated with acid sulfate soils are anticipated.
